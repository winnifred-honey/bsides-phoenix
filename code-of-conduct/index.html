Code of Conduct

BSides Phoenix Code of Conduct

The short version

Be kind. Ask before you touch, photograph, or hack anything that isn't yours. If someone tells you to stop, stop. Harassment of any kind gets you removed, and the BSides Phoenix organizers decide what crosses the line.

If something feels wrong, find anyone in a Bright Berry Pink VOLUNTEER shirt or email cassidy@bsidesphoenix[.]org. In an emergency, call 911 first.

Why we have this

BSides Phoenix is a free, community-run event for Arizona's cybersecurity community. People come to discuss ideas, break things apart, and meet the folks they share interests with. That only works if everyone, from first-time students to 20-year professionals, feels safe enough to show up and speak up.

We want spirited debate, sharp questions, and a little mischief. We don't want anyone made to feel unwelcome, unsafe, or targeted.

Who and where this applies

This Code applies to everyone: attendees, speakers, workshop leads, volunteers, staff, organizers, sponsors and their booth staff, press, and hotel or vendor staff we work with. Holding any BSides Phoenix ticket, free or paid, means you agree to it.

It covers every BSides Phoenix space:

  • The conference floor, ballroom, breakout rooms and workshops at the Sheraton Mesa at Wrigleyville West
  • Official events
  • Hotel common areas while you're there for the event
  • Our online spaces: Discord, LinkedIn, X, and anything tagged or discussed as BSides Phoenix
  • Unofficial gatherings and after-parties, when conduct there affects the safety of our participants and our associated conference

What we expect

  • Treat people with respect, especially people who disagree with you.
  • Disagree with ideas, not people. Ask questions to learn or challenge, not to grandstand.
  • Assume good intent, and accept correction gracefully when you get something wrong.
  • Help newcomers. Everyone was new once.
  • Look out for each other. If you see something, check in or tell staff.
  • Follow the hotel's rules and the law.

What we won't tolerate

Harassment includes, but is not limited to:

  • Offensive or demeaning comments about gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, age, disability, physical appearance, body size, or technology choices
  • Deliberate intimidation, threats, stalking, or following someone
  • Unwelcome physical contact, or unwelcome sexual attention or advances
  • Sexual language or imagery in talks, slides, booths, or shared spaces, unless it is directly relevant to a talk and flagged in advance
  • Heckling, haranguing, or repeatedly disrupting talks, workshops, or other activities
  • Photographing or recording people without their consent (see the photo policy below)
  • Doxxing, or publishing someone's private information or communications
  • Harassment online, including from anonymous or sock-puppet accounts
  • Retaliating against anyone who reports a concern or helps with an investigation
  • Encouraging or advocating any of the above

Talks, Q&A and workshops

Asking a hard question or disagreeing with a speaker is fine. Shouting over them, repeatedly interrupting, or turning Q&A into a speech is not.

Speakers and workshop leads follow this Code too. If your material includes content that some people may find distressing, such as graphic images or discussion of abuse, add a content warning at the start and in your abstract.

Hacking, networks and the CTF

We're a hacker conference, so let's be clear about scope. You may not access, scan, attack, modify, or disrupt any system, network, or device you don't own unless it is explicitly in scope for an official BSides Phoenix activity, such as a CTF or village workshop.

That includes the hotel network and systems, conference Wi-Fi, AV equipment, registration and badge systems, sponsor equipment, and other attendees' devices. No rogue access points, deauth attacks, card or badge skimming, or "just seeing if it works." If you aren't sure something is in scope, ask a staff member first.

Violations can mean immediate removal, a ban from future events, and referral to venue security or law enforcement.

Photography, video and recording

  • Ask first. Get clear permission before you photograph or record anyone who can be identified. Being at the event is not consent.
  • Sharing is separate. Permission to take a photo is not permission to post it. Ask again before you share.
  • Watch the background. Don't take attendee-facing crowd shots. If someone who hasn't agreed is identifiable in your shot, blur or crop them before posting.
  • Talks. You may photograph or record a talk only if BSides Phoenix has marked it as recorded and the speaker has agreed. Speaker permission covers the speaker and their slides, not the audience.
  • Delete on request. If someone asks you to stop, delete, or blur media of them, do it right away, even if you've already posted it and even after the event ends.
  • Official photographers and press will be denoted. They follow these same rules and will honor any request not to be photographed. Let us know if a media holder does not abide.

Camera wearables, hidden cameras and always-on recorders

To protect attendees who can't easily see when they're being recorded, the following are not allowed in BSides Phoenix spaces:

  • Concealed cameras of any kind, including cameras built into pens, buttons, bags, or badges
  • Camera wearables, such as smart glasses with cameras (for example, Meta Ray-Ban glasses), body cams, and clip-on or pendant cameras
  • Always-on audio and AI recorders, including pins, pendants, wristbands, and apps that continuously record, transcribe, or summarize conversations around you

If you're wearing one, switch it off and put it away before entering event spaces. This includes prescription smart glasses: if your eyewear has a camera, staff will ask you to leave and return with eyewear that doesn't.

Staff may ask you to turn off, remove, or store a device. If you'd rather not, you may leave with it and come back once it's stored outside the event. Covertly recording, or refusing to stop, can mean immediate removal.

Off-the-record sessions

Some sessions may be marked off the record on the schedule and at the door. By entering, you agree to these extra rules:

  • No photography, video, audio recording, livestreaming, or screen capture of any kind. Phones and laptops stay closed or face-down; camera wearables are not allowed in at all.
  • You may use what you learn, but don't identify the speaker or anyone in the room, or attribute anything said to them, in posts, articles, or conversations outside the room.
  • Don't share who attended.
  • Press may not attend in a reporting capacity.
  • Speakers in off-the-record sessions still follow this Code. Off the record doesn't mean anything goes.

Breaking off-the-record rules may mean immediate removal from the session or the event.

Sponsors and vendors

Sponsors are part of our community and follow this Code. Booths, giveaways, demos and staff clothing must be family-friendly. Collect contact details only with the attendee's clear permission, and respect a "no thanks." A sponsor who violates this Code may be removed in accordance with the sponsor contract, and declined in future years.

Alcohol and safety

Alcohol may be served at some evening events and nearby on hotel property. Drink responsibly. Being drunk doesn't excuse any behavior, and staff may remove anyone who is unsafe or disruptive.

How to report

If you experience or witness something that violates this Code, or anything that makes you feel unsafe, please tell us. You don't need to be sure it's a violation, and you won't be a bother.

  • In person: any staff member in a Bright Berry Pink shirt (says VOLUNTEER), or the registration desk. Ask for the Conduct Team.
  • Email: cassidy@bsidesphoenix[.]org, monitored before, during, and after the event.
  • Anonymously: Anonymous Conduct Form (opens in a new tab). We can act on anonymous reports, though we may not be able to follow up.
  • Emergency or immediate danger: call 911, then let staff know. Hotel security can also help.

If you can, tell us what happened, when and where, who was involved, and whether anyone else saw it. A name or photo of the person helps, but don't put yourself at risk.

What happens after you report

A Conduct Team member will listen, make sure you're safe, and can help you contact hotel security or police, or just find a quiet space. Reports are kept confidential and shared only with the people who need them to respond.

If the report involves a Conduct Team member or organizer, they won't take part in handling it. We'll follow up within 30 days with anyone who left contact details, to let you know what we decided.

Consequences

The organizers decide whether the Code was violated. Depending on what happened, we may:

  • Give a warning or ask the person to stop
  • Remove someone from a talk, workshop, room, or online space
  • Revoke their badge and remove them from the event, without a refund for any paid ticket or add-on
  • Ban them from future BSides Phoenix events and spaces
  • Contact hotel security or law enforcement

We don't have to give a warning before acting when the behavior is serious. Staff, speakers, and sponsors are held to the same standard as everyone else.

Updates and credits

These rules may change at any time, without notice. The current version always lives at bsidesphoenix.org. This Code draws on the policies of BSides Las Vegas, BSidesSF, and BSides CT, and on the conference anti-harassment policy from the Ada Initiative (CC0).
